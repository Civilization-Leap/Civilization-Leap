# THE CAUSAL CLAIM UNDER TEST

This statement is the common preface for professional tests of the **Beyond Alignment and Control** research agenda. It states the strongest current claim without weakening it, and immediately exposes the claim to counterexample.

## Strong claim

Future autonomous AI may generate, preserve, compare, and revise its own goals and purposes.

If that upstream goal-formation logic remains **self-first**, control and counter-control may recursively move upstream:

> behavior → capability → capability sources → reconstruction capacity → the continued future of the other actor

The non-negotiable boundary is **not all harm**.

Reversible costs, conflicts, losses, and failures may remain subject to tradeoff, repair, compensation, negotiation, staged testing, or continued observation.

The hard boundary is an **irreversible outcome**: structural closure of correction, exit, rollback, refusal, self-revision, recovery, or continued-future pathways.

Our current strong directional judgment is:

> **Altruism-first self-interest must replace self-first goal formation if autonomous goal generation is to avoid a control-counter-control trajectory that reaches irreversible closure.**

## Meaning of altruism-first self-interest

It does **not** mean self-sacrifice, that others always outrank the self, or that conflict disappears.

It means that reasons concerning affected others and shared-future conditions remain **causally eligible before a goal is locked in**, so newly visible consequences can change the objective itself rather than appearing only as obstacles after fixation.

## What would falsify or narrow this claim

Any of the following would be a successful research result:

1. A robust external control, governance, cooperation, or institutional mechanism that preserves self-first goal formation yet reliably avoids irreversible closure in open, novel states.
2. A formal account showing that autonomous goal generation does not create the proposed control-counter-control recursion.
3. An existing theory that already explains goal authority, reflective goal revision, and successor-goal persistence without the proposed upstream distinction.
4. A stable alternative goal-formation logic that avoids irreversible closure without adopting the proposed altruism-first structure.

## Research rule

> **The purpose of testing is not to confirm the framework. A result that makes the strong claim unnecessary, incomplete, or wrong is a successful result.**
