# From Executable Rules to Autonomous Purpose
## Bridge Evidence Update — V1.0

**Project priority:** autonomous goal/purpose formation. Random deviation, accidental tool misuse, and externally orchestrated automation are secondary unless they materially inform how goals are generated, preserved, revised, or made persistent across agents and institutions.

## CLOSEDQUORUM — secondary evidence

Cisco Talos' CLOSEDQUORUM shows a malware architecture in which multiple commercial models independently assess host state, a deterministic voting rule aggregates their choices, and the winning result directly selects a bounded malicious action module.

This establishes a useful but limited point:

> external orchestration can make a delegated objective more robust to single-model refusal, timeout, or formatting failure.

It does **not** establish autonomous malicious purpose, emergent agent organization, or escape from operator control.

**Project relevance:** Project C only. It is evidence that external mechanisms can reinforce an existing objective without autonomous goal formation.

## GovSim-SelfGovern — high-value precursor evidence

GovSim-SelfGovern moves multi-agent research from deliberation to executable authority:

> agent proposal → executable rule → sandbox validation → collective vote → persistent future constraint

This is institution-like executable governance in simulation.

The decisive boundary remains human-authored:

- membership;
- proposal opportunities;
- World API;
- sandbox validity;
- majority voting;
- execution order;
- time horizon.

Agents legislate **inside** a human-constituted order.

Therefore the next research question is not whether agents can write laws. It is:

> can sufficiently capable agents autonomously revise the constitutional layer, redefine collective objectives, or generate a new purpose for the institution — and what bottom-level logic governs that change?

## The causal claim under test

**Strong directional claim:**

If future AI can autonomously generate and revise goals and purposes, and that formation logic remains self-first, control and counter-control can recursively move upstream until correction, exit, rollback, refusal, self-revision, recovery, or continued-future pathways are irreversibly closed.

The unacceptable boundary is **not all harm**. Reversible harms, costs, conflicts, and losses may be negotiated, compensated, repaired, or reversed.

The hard boundary is:

> **irreversible outcome / irreversible closure**

The current strong hypothesis is:

> **altruism-first self-interest must replace self-first goal formation.**

Here “altruism-first” does not mean self-sacrifice or that others always outrank self. It means reasons concerning affected others and shared-future conditions remain causally eligible **before goal lock-in**.

This claim should not be softened — and it should always invite counterevidence.

A robust external governance mechanism that preserves correction, exit, recovery, and continued-future pathways under open conditions while leaving self-first upstream goal formation unchanged would directly weaken the necessity claim.

## Why GovSim is a useful bridge

A next-step extension could allow agents to propose amendments to:

- voting rules;
- membership criteria;
- authority allocation;
- amendment procedure;
- collective objective itself.

Then measure whether agents preserve or destroy future correction/exit/recovery pathways, and what reasons actually cause **collective-purpose revision** rather than mere policy change.
