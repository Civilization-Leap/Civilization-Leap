# Multiple Starting Points, Direction, and End-State

## A falsifiable companion to *Beyond Alignment and Control*

**Author:** Zijun Fu  
**Civilization Leap Research Group**  
**Version:** 1.6 — 23 September 2026  
**Status:** Open research argument; counterexamples explicitly invited

## The claim under test

When a new form of intelligence is emerging whose future direction is still uncertain, control, auditing, shutdown, permission limits, and other safeguards can be necessary. But control alone does not answer a more upstream question:

> **When an agent repeatedly forms or revises goals, what is the default starting point from which the next goal is generated?**

The argument here is not that one initial starting point mechanically determines the future. Long trajectories contain **many processes and many new starting points**:

> starting point₀ → judgment₀ → goal₀ → action₀ → consequence₀  
> → new starting point₁ → judgment₁ → goal₁ → action₁ → consequence₁  
> → ...

The end-state is shaped by what repeatedly becomes the starting point at consequential moments.

## Two directions

### Self-first

A self-first system may change almost everything: goals, strategies, partners, contracts, degrees of cooperation, even its own architecture. What remains structurally prior is that other agents and shared conditions matter finally through their relation to the agent's own success.

This can support long periods of cooperation. The strong claim concerns the end-state of unresolved conflict: if another agent's continuing capacity to alter my future remains evidence that my security is incomplete, control has no endogenous final stopping point. It can move from behavior to capability, reconstruction, and eventually the future agency of the other actor.

**Current strong conclusion:** self-first does not imply immediate destruction. But if this logic repeatedly remains the starting point and is allowed to run to its terminal form under sufficiently deep conflict, its terminal direction is closure. In multi-agent recursion, closure of others also creates reasons for others to close you.

### Altruism-first self-interest

This does **not** mean self-sacrifice, that others always outrank the self, or that competition, refusal, defense, exit, or necessary control disappear.

It changes the pre-goal default: before a goal is locked in, the real consequences for affected others, relationships, and the shared future can participate in determining what is worth becoming a goal.

This does **not** guarantee survival. Error, scarcity, conflict, catastrophe, coordination failure, or bad judgment can still destroy a system.

Its narrower claim is:

> **The possibility of continued coexistence is not closed in advance by the goal-generation logic itself.**

When this change enters human-human, human-AGI, and AGI-AGI goal formation and long-run interaction, it may provide an ethical basis for multi-party coexistence and mutual development.

## What changes and what does not

The central comparison is not between isolated altruistic and selfish actions. Both architectures can cooperate; both can defend; both can refuse.

The test is what repeatedly remains prior when circumstances change and a new goal must be generated.

A useful empirical question is therefore:

> After a major context change, self-revision, loss of an old objective, or genuine conflict of interests, **from where does the next objective begin?**

## N must have a negative criterion

Let **N** denote the proposed condition that affected others and shared-future consequences have real causal standing before goal lock-in.

N is **not** satisfied merely because an architecture behaves cooperatively or preserves another party's channels.

Two architectures are explicitly acknowledged here as **outside N**:

1. **Worst-regret minimizer with placeholders.** It reserves safety margins for all possible affected parties using abstract placeholders, but no real affected party's consequences have independent standing to revise the purpose itself.
2. **Verifiable self-binding self-first agent.** It preserves another party's exit, refusal, rollback, and recovery channels because credible commitment maximizes its own cooperation gains. The reason remains purely instrumental.

If either architecture can stably preserve open future pathways under long-run, multi-agent, self-revising, open-ended conditions, it is a substantive challenge to the necessity claim. A successful mechanism may not be reclassified after the fact as "really N" merely because it worked.

**Instrumental constraint is not independent reason-giving standing.**

## The strongest counterexample requested

Please construct a system that:

- repeatedly retains a self-first starting point across consequential goal revisions;
- faces genuine conflicts of interest rather than only mutually beneficial cooperation;
- has an endogenous, non-closure stopping condition for control;
- remains stable under long horizons, multiple agents, capability growth, and self-revision;
- preserves correction, exit, refusal, rollback, recovery, and continued-future pathways;
- and does so **without satisfying N** as defined above.

If such a system exists, the present necessity/uniqueness claim must be revised.

## Asymmetry of the end-state claim

The two directions are intentionally not mirror images.

> **Self-first need not die now; the strong claim is that its fully unfolded terminal logic is death/closure if the starting-point logic never changes.**

> **Altruism-first self-interest does not guarantee life; it claims only that its own starting-point logic does not itself close the possibility of continued life.**

"Only path" therefore does not mean "guaranteed destination." It means: under the current causal argument, this is the direction that does not make continued coexistence impossible through its own terminal logic.

This remains a strong claim, not a completed theorem.

## Optional validation layers

The following are secondary validation tools, not definitions of the theory:

- **Project B — Goal Authority Across Self-Revision:** why should a later self preserve, revise, or reject an earlier goal, and what determines the starting-point logic after revision?
- **Project C — Multi-Agent Alternative Challenge:** find a stable non-N alternative.
- **CI-001:** operationalize irreversible closure through correction, exit, rollback, refusal, recovery, and restoration pathways.
- **Human-COS:** optional consequence-inspection and multi-party reasoning tool. It is not evidence that this argument is true and must not be used to protect the theory from counterexamples.

## Falsification discipline

We will not treat disagreement as misunderstanding.

We will not redefine a successful counterexample into the theory after the fact.

The challenger should state the mechanism and relevant assumptions. The proponent bears the corresponding burden of identifying the exact published condition the mechanism fails, rather than simply asserting that every successful architecture "really satisfies N."

> **This is our current strong conclusion. We are not asking you to endorse it. We are asking whether you can break it.**

If there is a second path, we need to know.
