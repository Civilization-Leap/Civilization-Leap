# Research Projects: Beyond Alignment and Control

These project briefs turn the paper **Beyond Alignment and Control: Autonomous Goal and Purpose Formation as the Upstream Problem of Human-AGI Coexistence** into three independent research questions that can support formal, empirical, conceptual, or governance work.

**Permanent parent work:** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22858094  
**EA Forum discussion:** https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/tTrdYWNeuazkm7aLt/beyond-alignment-and-control-why-autonomous-goal-formation  
**Public discussion issue:** https://github.com/Civilization-Leap/Civilization-Leap/issues/3

## Project A — Control Stopping Conditions

**Question:** Under what formal or empirical conditions can a safety intervention have a bounded stopping point rather than recursively moving upstream from behavior to capability, reconstruction capacity, and eventually the other actor's continuing agency?

Best fit: agent foundations, causal incentives, formal decision theory, corrigibility, dynamic games, AI governance.

A result that finds a robust bounded stopping rule without changing upstream goal formation would weaken a central necessity claim of the parent framework.

## Project B — Goal Authority Across Self-Revision

**Question:** What makes a reason remain causally eligible to preserve, disqualify, or replace a goal across repeated goal revision and self-modification, and how can this be distinguished from policy adaptation, world-model updating, prompt effects, or memory retrieval?

Best fit: self-modifying agents, reflective agency, formal decision theory, AI evaluation, philosophy of action, normative alignment.

If existing frameworks already fully capture this distinction, the proposed research object should be retired rather than protected by terminology.

## Project C — Multi-Agent Alternative Challenge

**Question:** Can bargaining, cooperative AI, credible commitments, institutional design, or other multi-agent mechanisms preserve multiple agents' continued futures and prevent irreversible harm without changing the upstream logic by which agents form and preserve their own goals?

Best fit: cooperative AI, multi-agent systems, game theory, mechanism design, agent governance, AI strategy.

This is deliberately designed as the strongest challenge to the parent framework. A robust external mechanism that preserves multiple agents' futures, prevents irreversible harm, survives major capability shifts, and does so while leaving upstream goal formation self-first would be a direct counterexample.

## Research standard

These projects do **not** assume that current AI systems possess autonomous final purposes or moral subjecthood. They do not weaken shutdown, isolation, auditing, red-teaming, evaluation, or permission controls in order to make the theory come out true.

A result that contradicts the framework is a successful research result.
